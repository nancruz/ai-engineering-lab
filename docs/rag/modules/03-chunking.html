Cut documents where meaning allows

Choosing the unit of retrieval

Why not retrieve whole documents?

Modules 1 and 2 searched whole documents. That works on 24 short files, but it doesn't scale:

  • Cost and limits. Every retrieved token goes into the prompt, and you pay for it on every question. A 40-page handbook doesn't fit.
  • Precision. A long document matches many queries a little. A focused section matches the right query a lot.
  • Vectors blur. One embedding of a whole document averages all its topics into a single direction.

Chunks too small have the opposite problem: "Roll back immediately." means something very different in staging and in production. A chunk needs enough context to be understood on its own, because that's how the model will see it.

Three ways to cut

Fixed windows

Every N characters, optionally overlapping. Simple, predictable sizes, and blind to structure: it cuts mid-sentence and mid-table. Overlap reduces the damage at the cost of duplicated text.

Paragraphs

Split on blank lines and pack whole paragraphs into chunks up to N characters. Sentences survive; a section's heading may still end up in a different chunk from its content.

Headings (structure-aware)

One chunk per Markdown section, carrying its heading path ("Who approves"). Long sections are packed by paragraph. Uses the structure the author already wrote.

Add context to every chunk

Prefix the document title and section heading to the text you index, so "If the check fails…" is indexed as part of "Playback rollback runbook › EU production". This course does that in Chunk.search_text().

Keep provenance

Every chunk in minirag records its document id, heading path and exact character offsets: doc.body[chunk.start:chunk.end] == chunk.text. That one property gives you citations that point to the exact passage, highlighting in a UI, and re-indexing only what changed (Module 7). Don't throw it away by cleaning text after splitting.

There's no universally best chunk size. It depends on your documents, your questions and your model's context window. Common starting points are 200–1,000 tokens. Pick one with an evaluation; in Module 5 you'll compare these strategies on 28 labelled questions.

Key takeaways

  • Chunking trades precision and cost (small) against self-contained meaning (large).
  • Structure-aware chunking keeps rules with their conditions; adding title and heading context helps both search and the model.
  • Exact offsets make citations, highlighting and incremental indexing possible.
BUILD

Lab 3: three chunkers with exact offsets

Goal: implement fixed, paragraph and heading-aware chunking, all returning spans whose text you can reconstruct exactly.

You write, in minirag/chunking.py: fixed_spans, paragraph_spans, pack_spans, section_spans. Supplied: headings(), strip_span(), and chunk_document(), which wires them together.

All four functions work on spans, (start, end) offsets into the text, rather than on substrings. That's what keeps provenance exact.

  1. Fixed windows

    fixed_spans(text, start, end, size, overlap): windows of size characters, each starting size - overlap after the previous one, stopping after the window that reaches end. Skip windows that are only whitespace.

    $ python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_lab3_chunking -k FixedWindows

  2. Paragraphs and packing

    paragraph_spans finds paragraphs between blank lines (the PARAGRAPH_BREAK regex is supplied) and strips them. pack_spans merges consecutive spans while the merged span fits in size, and cuts any single span that's too long into fixed windows.

    $ python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_lab3_chunking -k Paragraphs
    Hint

    PARAGRAPH_BREAK.finditer(text, start, end) searches only inside the range and returns offsets into text. A paragraph runs from the end of one break to the start of the next.

  3. Sections

    section_spans(body): a section runs from one heading to the next. Use the supplied headings(body), which already ignores # lines inside code blocks. Drop sections that contain only their heading line; their name survives in the heading paths of their subsections.

    $ python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_lab3_chunking

  4. Look at the chunks

    $ python3 -m minirag chunks playback-rollback-runbook --chunks fixed --size 300
$ python3 -m minirag chunks playback-rollback-runbook --chunks headings
    playback-rollback-runbook: 6 chunks · strategy=headings size=800 overlap=0

── playback-rollback-runbook#1  [194:558]  364 chars  · When to roll back
   ## When to roll back Roll back production when either condition is true: - The canary …
── playback-rollback-runbook#2  [560:895]  335 chars  · Who approves
   ...

  5. Search with chunks

    From now on, --chunks headings (800 characters) is the default. Compare what reaches the top for a question about the EU rule:

    $ python3 -m minirag search "anything extra to check before rolling back playback in the EU" --chunks fixed --size 300 --k 3
$ python3 -m minirag search "anything extra to check before rolling back playback in the EU" --k 3

    With fixed windows the top hit is chunk #5: it starts mid-word inside Steps and stops at "do not roll back"; the words "in the EU region" are in chunk #6. With headings, the whole EU production section is one chunk and comes first.

Experiments

  • Size sweepRun chunks on-call-handbook with --chunks headings --size 200, 400 and 1200. How many chunks? Do any read badly on their own?
    Look for: the size where chunks stop being self-contained. Keep a note; you'll check it against numbers in Module 5.
  • Context headersEdit Chunk.search_text() to return only self.text, then rerun the EU search above with --chunks headings. Put it back afterwards.
    Look for: how much the title and heading in the indexed text contribute to ranking. This is the cheap version of Anthropic's "contextual retrieval", which has an LLM write that context.
  • Tables and codeChunk playback-error-codes with --chunks fixed --size 200. What happens to the table rows?
    Look for: a table row separated from its header row loses its column meanings. Structure-aware chunkers often treat tables and code blocks as units that can't be split.

Done when python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_lab3_chunking passes: every chunk reconstructs exactly, respects the size limit, and the EU rule keeps its heading.
