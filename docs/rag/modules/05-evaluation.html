Turn "seems better" into numbers

You'll learn the retrieval metrics everyone uses, how to check answers, and when to trust an LLM as a judge, then build a harness that scores your whole system in one command.

Measure retrieval and answers separately

Start with the questions, not the metric

An evaluation set is a list of realistic questions with labels written before you look at what the system returns. The course's questions.json labels each question with:

LabelExample (q02)Used for
relevant["catalog-api-timeouts"]document-level retrieval metrics
evidence["800 ms total deadline"]did the answering passage reach the prompt?
must_include / must_not_include["800"] / ["3000"]cheap, deterministic answer checks
expectedanswer or abstaindoes it refuse when it should?
typefreshnesssee where it fails: exact, paraphrase, multi-doc, access, missing…

Questions are split into dev (28: tune on these) and test (6: look once, at the end). If you tune until the test set looks good, it has become a dev set and tells you nothing about new questions.

Retrieval metrics

Retrieval returns a ranked list; the labels say which items are relevant. Four standard metrics read that list differently. Rearrange the ranking below and watch them disagree.

Hit@k and recall@k

Hit asks "anything relevant in the top k?" and is forgiving. Recall asks "what share of the relevant items?" and matters when an answer needs two documents.

MRR and nDCG

Both reward putting relevant items first. MRR only looks at the first hit; nDCG gives every relevant item credit, discounted by position (1/log₂(position + 1)).

Document-level metrics can look perfect while the answer is still missing: the right document was found, but not the section that answers the question. That's why this course adds evidence found: the share of a question's evidence strings that appear inside one of the retrieved chunks. It's the most honest retrieval number here.

Checking answers

Two complementary approaches:

  • Deterministic checks: required facts present (800), forbidden facts absent (3000, 720 for an engineer), abstained when it should have, cites a relevant document. Fast, free, repeatable. They miss paraphrases ("eight hundred milliseconds") and can't judge reasoning.
  • LLM-as-judge: give a model the sources and the answer and ask, claim by claim, whether the sources support it. That measures faithfulness, the core RAG failure: fluent answers that the sources don't support.

Judges are models too. They prefer longer answers, can favour their own model family, and drift when you change their prompt. Before trusting a judge, label 10–20 answers yourself and check that the judge agrees with you. Ask for per-claim yes/no decisions rather than a 1–10 score; they're more consistent.

Reading results honestly

  • Look at failures, not just averages. Ten minutes reading wrong answers teaches more than a decimal place.
  • Small sets are noisy. With 28 questions, one question moves an average by 0.036. Don't celebrate 0.02.
  • Slice by type. A change that fixes paraphrases and breaks exact codes can leave the average unchanged.

Key takeaways

  • Write labelled questions first; split dev and test; slice results by question type.
  • Hit, recall, MRR and nDCG measure document ranking; evidence found measures whether the answer passage arrived.
  • Combine deterministic answer checks with an LLM judge that you've checked against your own labels.
BUILD

Lab 5: an evaluation harness

Goal: one command that scores any retriever and LLM configuration on the labelled questions, broken down by question type, with failures listed.

You write, in minirag/evaluation.py: hit_at_k, recall_at_k, reciprocal_rank, ndcg_at_k, evidence_recall, evaluate_retrieval, check_answer, parse_judgement. Supplied: the CLI report, evaluate_answers, judge_faithfulness and the judge prompt.

  1. Ranking metrics

    Implement the four metrics. Each returns None when the question has no relevant documents, only counts the first k unique ids, and returns floats.

    $ python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_lab5_evaluation -k RankingMetrics

  2. Evidence

    evidence_recall(texts, evidence): the share of evidence strings found, case-insensitively, inside a single retrieved chunk text.

    $ python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_lab5_evaluation -k Evidence

  3. The harness

    evaluate_retrieval(retriever, questions, k): retrieve for every question with its role, turn hits into unique document ids, compute each metric plus evidence and tokens, list any leaked documents, and summarize. Leaks should always be zero: if not, your Module 4 filter has a hole.

    $ python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_lab5_evaluation -k Harness

  4. Answer checks and the judge

    check_answer applies the labels to an Answer. parse_judgement pulls the JSON out of a judge's reply (models often wrap it in prose or code fences) and computes the supported share of claims.

    $ python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_lab5_evaluation

  5. Evaluate

Experiments

  • Beat the baseline with a real modelpython3 -m minirag eval --answers --show-failures --llm ollama:llama3.2:3b (or a hosted model). Write down pass, abstain and cites.
    Look for: which failures disappear (multi-doc, paraphrase) and which new ones appear (wrong abstentions, forbidden facts). If a model leaks 720 to an engineer, check your filter: it never should.
  • Tune chunking with numbersRun eval --k 3 with --chunks fixed --size 300, --chunks paragraph --size 600, --chunks headings and --chunks document.
    Look for: evidence vs tokens. Pick the configuration you'd ship and say why in one sentence. That's the decision this harness exists for.
  • Re-check Module 1's ideasRe-run your BM25 --b values and your stemmer from Lab 1, this time with eval.
    Look for: whether a change that fixed one query improves the whole set, or just moves failures around.
  • Judge the judgeRun eval --answers --llm <model> --judge <model> --out runs/judged.json. Open the file, pick 10 answers, and decide yourself whether each claim is supported.
    Look for: how often you and the judge agree, and in which direction it errs. Would you let it block a release?
  • The test set, onceWhen you've chosen a configuration, run it with --split test.
    Look for: a drop from dev to test is normal. A large drop means you tuned to the dev questions.

Done when python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_lab5_evaluation passes and minirag eval --answers prints both tables for your system.

GO FURTHER

Optional

  • Add a bootstrap confidence interval to eval: resample questions with replacement 1,000 times and report the 5th and 95th percentile of the difference between two configurations.
  • Generate extra questions with an LLM from random chunks ("write a question this passage answers"), then review them by hand. Synthetic data is cheap; unreviewed synthetic data is misleading.
  • Try an evaluation library such as Ragas on your saved runs and compare its faithfulness scores with yours.
